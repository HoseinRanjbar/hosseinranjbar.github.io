SIGN LANGUAGE · HUMAN MOTION · GENERATIVE AI

Hi, I’m Hossein.

I study how AI can understand and generate human signing.

I’m a PhD researcher at Université Paris-Saclay, in the Sign Language Group at LISN, supervised by Michael Filhol. My research focuses on sign language synthesis with generative AI, with the goal of producing natural, expressive signing in French Sign Language (LSF).

Previously, I was a Research Associate at the Chair of Language, Technology and Accessibility at the University of Zurich, where I worked on sign language synthesis and AI-assisted sign language video editing under the supervision of Prof. Sarah Ebling. I received my MSc in Mechatronics Engineering from Sharif University of Technology, where I conducted research in the Social and Cognitive Robotics Lab under the supervision of Dr. Alireza Taheri. My master’s thesis focused on continuous sign language recognition, through which I gained experience in computer vision, natural language processing, and graph neural networks. I also received my BSc in Mechanical Engineering from Sharif University of Technology, where my undergraduate project focused on biomechanics, specifically estimating loads on the human spine.

My work brings together computer vision, language, and motion—from recognizing signs to generating them.

What I’m working on

My PhD explores generative models for natural sign language motion. I’m especially interested in generating motion between sparse keyframes while preserving the hand shapes, timing, and facial expressions that carry linguistic meaning.
